Reading is not practice. Practice is practice. These are the platforms where you can actually do the work.

## Certification bodies

- **[CompTIA](https://www.comptia.org/)** — vendor-neutral foundational certifications: A+, Network+, Security+, CySA+, PenTest+, CASP+. Strong HR filter, especially in government and DoD-adjacent work.
- **[EC-Council](https://www.eccouncil.org/)** — issuer of the Certified Ethical Hacker (CEH) and related certifications (CHFI, ECSA, LPT). Common requirement in defense, federal, and many corporate roles.
- The [Reading List](/resources/reading-list/) covers what to study; this page covers where to practice.

## Free or strong free tier

- **[TryHackMe](https://tryhackme.com/)** — guided rooms, beginner-friendly, good free tier. The best on-ramp I've found.
- **[Hack The Box](https://www.hackthebox.com/)** — broader and harder. Free machines available alongside the paid VIP tier.
- **[PortSwigger Web Security Academy](https://portswigger.net/web-security)** — free, structured, the best web security training that exists at any price. Work through it cover to cover.
- **[OverTheWire](https://overthewire.org/)** — wargames. Bandit is the canonical Linux fluency drill.
- **[picoCTF](https://picoctf.org/)** — CTF challenges aimed at beginners; serious people use it too.
- **[Cyber Defenders](https://cyberdefenders.org/)** — blue-team scenarios with provided artifacts.
- **[Blue Team Labs Online](https://blueteamlabs.online/)** — defender-focused practice.
- **[Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/)** — surprisingly good free training for Azure security, defender products, and AD fundamentals.
- **[INE Free Training](https://ine.com/learning)** — free tier covers serious ground in networking and security.

## Paid, worth it for the right person

- **[Offensive Security / OSCP](https://www.offsec.com/)** — hands-on cert, respected, hard. Don't attempt without prior pentesting practice.
- **[HTB Academy](https://academy.hackthebox.com/)** — structured paths into HTB content.
- **[SANS](https://www.sans.org/)** — best-in-class content, premium pricing. If your employer pays, take advantage.
- **[Pentester Academy](https://www.pentesteracademy.com/)** — AD attacks especially.
- **[Antisyphon Training](https://www.antisyphontraining.com/)** — pay-what-you-can model, quality instructors.
- **[ITPro TV](https://www.itpro.tv/)** (now part of ACI Learning) — video-based cert prep across CompTIA, Cisco, Microsoft, and vendor-neutral security tracks. Good for passive learning to complement hands-on work; subscription model.

## CTF events

- **[CTFtime](https://ctftime.org/)** — the calendar. Pick an event, form or join a team, play.
- **National Cyber League (NCL)** — US students; structured competition.
- **Various conference CTFs** — DEF CON, BSides events, others. Worth tracking even if you don't compete.

## Bug bounty platforms

- **HackerOne, Bugcrowd, Intigriti** — the major Western platforms. Pick one to start; don't spread thin.
- **YesWeHack** — strong in Europe.
- **Self-managed programs** — many companies run their own. Often less crowded.

## How to actually use these

- **Don't sample.** Pick one platform and work through a real path on it before judging.
- **Take notes as you go.** A finished room you can't explain isn't learning.
- **Write up what you learn.** Even if you don't publish, the act of writing locks it in.
- **Time-box.** Spending six hours stuck on a single box is sometimes a learning experience and sometimes wasted effort. Know which.
