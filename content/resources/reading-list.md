Books that have earned their place on my shelf, or that I tell people about when they ask. No affiliate links. No "ten books to become a hacker." Just things I've found useful.

## OccupyTheWeb / Hackers-Arise series

OccupyTheWeb ("OTW", "Master OTW") writes the most accessible on-ramp into practical hacking that I'm aware of. His books are short, hands-on, and avoid the textbook style that puts beginners to sleep. If someone asks me how to start, this series — in roughly the order below — is what I recommend.

- **[Linux Basics for Hackers, 2nd Edition](https://nostarch.com/linux-basics-hackers-2nd-edition)** (No Starch Press) — the canonical starting point. Kali Linux orientation, command-line fluency, networking, scripting, and a new chapter on AI in cybersecurity. If you read one OTW book, read this.
- **[Getting Started Becoming a Master Hacker](https://www.amazon.com/Getting-Started-Becoming-Master-Hacker/dp/1711729299)** (self-published) — broader survey of the field aimed at total beginners. A reasonable pair with *Linux Basics* if you want context for *why* you're learning each piece.
- **[Network Basics for Hackers](https://www.amazon.com/Network-Basics-Hackers-Networks-Break/dp/B0BS3GZ1R9)** — TCP/IP, Wireshark and tcpdump, Linux firewalls, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, automotive, SCADA/ICS, and RF networks. Pairs naturally with our own [Networking](/education/networking/) page.
- **[Python Basics for Hackers](https://infosecpress.com/books/python-basics-for-hackers/)** (2025) — Python for cybersecurity applications. Aimed at people coming in with little or no coding background. Useful precisely because most "Python for security" books assume too much.
- **[Linux Basics for Hackers, 1st Edition](https://www.amazon.com/Linux-Basics-Hackers-Networking-Scripting/dp/1593278551)** (No Starch Press, 2018) — listed only because it's still widely circulated; prefer the 2nd edition for new purchases.

OTW also runs the [Hackers-Arise](https://hackers-arise.com/) training site, which is a useful companion to the books.

## Networking

- **TCP/IP Illustrated, Volume 1: The Protocols** — W. Richard Stevens & Kevin Fall. The book. Long, dense, repays the time.
- **Computer Networking: A Top-Down Approach** — Kurose & Ross. The standard university text. Solid.
- **Beej's Guide to Network Programming** — free online. Best short introduction to sockets.

## Linux

- **The Linux Command Line** — William Shotts. Free online. Excellent first book.
- **How Linux Works** — Brian Ward. Mental model for what's going on under the surface.
- **Linux Bible** — Christopher Negus. Wide reference if you prefer one big book to a shelf of small ones.

## Cybersecurity fundamentals

- **The Cuckoo's Egg** — Cliff Stoll. The original cybersecurity case study. Reads like a novel.
- **Sandworm** — Andy Greenberg. Modern nation-state attacks, well-reported.
- **This Is How They Tell Me the World Ends** — Nicole Perlroth. The zero-day market, told as journalism.

## Offensive

- **The Web Application Hacker's Handbook** — Stuttard & Pinto. Showing its age in places; still excellent for fundamentals.
- **Real-World Bug Hunting** — Peter Yaworski. Disclosed bug bounty writeups, organized by class.
- **The Hacker Playbook 3** — Peter Kim. Pragmatic offensive operations.
- **Red Team Field Manual** / **Blue Team Field Manual** — RTFM/BTFM. Pocket references.
- **Practical Malware Analysis** — Sikorski & Honig. Older but the fundamentals haven't changed.

## Defensive

- **The Practice of Network Security Monitoring** — Richard Bejtlich. Foundational for blue team thinking.
- **Crafting the InfoSec Playbook** — Murdoch, Bollinger, Hutchins. Detection engineering and response.
- **Intelligence-Driven Incident Response** — Roberts & Brown. How intel and IR fit together.

## Crypto

- **Serious Cryptography** — Jean-Philippe Aumasson. Modern, accessible, not a textbook.
- **Real-World Cryptography** — David Wong. Engineer-friendly, practical.

## Software, systems, and reasoning

- **The Pragmatic Programmer** — Hunt & Thomas. Engineering judgment.
- **Designing Data-Intensive Applications** — Martin Kleppmann. The book on modern systems.
- **Thinking, Fast and Slow** — Kahneman. Decision-making and bias. Relevant whether you realize it or not.

## Career and writing

- **The Pragmatic Programmer** (again) — chapter on continuous learning.
- **On Writing Well** — William Zinsser. Security writeups are writing. Make them readable.

## What I'd skip

- Anything titled *"Hacking for Beginners"* in 2026. The genre is mostly recycled.
- Books that promise certification pass in 30 days. They teach the test, not the topic.
- Vendor-published "thought leadership" PDFs. Marketing dressed up as research.
