If you're brand new to cybersecurity, the field looks like a wall of acronyms guarded by gatekeepers selling courses. It isn't. There's a path, and most people who succeed walk it in roughly the same order.

## What to do first

1. **Get comfortable in Linux.** Install Ubuntu or Debian in a VM and use it daily. Read [Linux Basics](/education/linux-basics/) for what "comfortable" means.
2. **Learn networking fundamentals.** TCP/IP, DNS, HTTP, subnetting, routing. See [Networking](/education/networking/).
3. **Pick up the security vocabulary.** CIA triad, authentication vs authorization, hashing vs encryption, common attack categories. The Security+ syllabus is a fine map even if you don't sit the exam.
4. **Practice in a structured environment.** TryHackMe and PortSwigger Web Security Academy both have excellent free tiers. Don't watch — do.

## What to do next

Once the fundamentals are solid, pick a track:

- **Defensive** — logging, SIEM, threat hunting. Build a Security Onion or Wazuh lab at home.
- **Offensive** — pentesting, web app testing, AD attacks. HTB and TryHackMe scale here.
- **AppSec** — secure code review, threat modeling. Start with the OWASP Top 10 in depth.
- **Cloud security** — AWS/Azure/GCP IAM and posture. Free tiers make this cheap to practice.
- **GRC** — risk, audit, frameworks. Less hands-on but very leverageable.

## What to skip

- "Top 10 hacking tools" videos. They teach nouns, not verbs.
- Bootcamp claims of becoming a security engineer in 30 days.
- Buying expensive courses before you've finished the free, high-quality ones.
